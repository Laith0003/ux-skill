# journey-mapping

Input rows: 51. Merged rows: 20.

Anecdote figures (map lifespans, discount examples, a single booking flow's step count) were dropped; step and phase counts that a check can use were kept.

## Evidence and framing

- **Build maps from research, and label any map that is not.** A map drawn from stakeholder or marketing opinion is a map of biases; build it from observed steps and feelings, with real users involved, and mark hypothesis maps (from analytics and the happy path) clearly as unverified so nobody mistakes them for findings. Confidence: medium. Sources: B30 p.241-256; B67 p.16, 20-22; B06 p.114-117; B11 p.73-75
- **Frame the map before drawing it.** Settle whose perspective it shows, how wide or deep it goes, what it emphasizes, how it is organized and what it will be used for; a single perspective per diagram generally makes it stronger. Confidence: medium. Sources: B64 p.42-49; B67 p.36-38
- **Follow the entity whose path fits the problem.** For a process shared by several roles, following the object (for example a rental car) through every hand-off can reveal more than following one person. Confidence: low. Sources: B67 p.28-29
- **Pick the diagram type by purpose.** Journey maps follow a customer deciding to buy and stay; service blueprints show real-time delivery including backstage work; experience maps place the organization inside a broader human activity; mental model diagrams group needs in a hierarchy instead of a timeline; spatial maps show where things happen. Confidence: low. Sources: B64 p.26, 33, 115, 117-118

## Structure

- **Map the whole experience in time order, from the customer's side.** Run left to right through before, during and after product use; a good checkout still fails if delivery or support fails; organize by the person's phases and moments, not by internal channels and touchpoints. Confidence: medium. Sources: B67 p.6-7, 16-17; B72 p.64, 132, 244; B96 p.74-75
- **Start with the core rows and add more only where research backs them.** The core set covers scope, goal, phases, actions, opportunities and pain points; depending on the data, add a mental-state line or emotions, thoughts, quotes as evidence, triggers, the channel or device at each step, touchpoints, barriers and effort. Confidence: medium. Sources: B67 p.10-12; B11 p.73-75; B17 p.159-160; B72 p.66-67; B96 p.74-75; B30 p.241-256; B64 p.25-26
- **Head each map with persona, scenario, trigger and goal.** State the user goal at the top and start from what triggers the journey; describe the context (place, trigger, time available, platform, emotion) because it drives requirements. Confidence: medium. Sources: B11 p.73-75; B17 p.159-160; B25 p.39
- **Keep maps simple: about 4 to 12 phases and no more than about 15 to 20 steps.** A first draft of 5 to 15 steps takes 30 to 60 minutes; past 20 steps the map is too complex, and customers describe in a handful of steps what internal teams draw in dozens. Confidence: medium. Sources: B64 p.164; B81 p.71-72; B34 p.181; B11 p.73-75
- **Show both sides of the value exchange.** An alignment diagram sets what the person does, thinks and feels against what the organization provides and runs, and treats each touchpoint as the place where value changes hands. Confidence: low. Sources: B64 p.25-26
- **Mark fail points, timings, emotion and roles on service blueprints.** Flag the steps where failure is likely, write the acceptable time for each interaction, show how strongly people feel, distinguish one-directional from two-directional contact, and label staff by their actual job. Confidence: low. Sources: B64 p.249, 251, 254, 264-265

## Finding opportunities

- **Hunt pain points at three levels and beyond the physical.** Check individual steps, runs of related steps and the journey as a whole; waiting states and workarounds (repeated refreshing, stuck sync screens) are pain points; social, identity, communication and emotional lenses surface the less obvious problems. Confidence: medium. Sources: B67 p.13-15; B72 p.66-67; B25 p.41; B96 p.74-75
- **Locate the moments of truth and design for them.** High-emotion points where people have already invested effort decide whether the relationship survives, and they are where people value improvement most. Confidence: low. Sources: B64 p.53-54
- **Draw a future-state map for each concept and plan the gap.** Re-map the experience with the proposed solution to expose missing requirements (setup, training, servicing), contrast it with the current map, and give each improvement a timeline, budget and owner. Confidence: medium. Sources: B67 p.22-24; B72 p.70-71; B06 p.114-117
- **Drop to task analysis for granular flows.** Put phases on top and detailed tasks with branches below; check each box answers why from above and how from below, with no more than 3 or 4 subgoals per goal. Confidence: medium. Sources: B67 p.27-28; B03 p.530-533
- **Read a tangled flow as a warning.** Sketch screens as boxes and branch points as triangles, and iterate until fewer branch points remain; a complicated flow predicts a hard interface. Confidence: low. Sources: B06 p.113-114
- **Keep maps alive.** Each fix or release alters the experience, so put map updates on the calendar. Confidence: low. Sources: B67 p.77-78

## Scenarios and stories

- **Derive situations, needs and goals before writing scenarios.** A situation triggers a need, which sets a goal; enumerate the situations for each persona, since each one can open one or several scenarios or journeys. Confidence: low. Sources: B50 p.91-92
- **Write scenarios with a scene, a goal, obstacles and an ending.** Establish the place and time of use, trace the persona's decisions and reasons, include before and after events, keep the user rather than the system driving the story, include negative paths and edge users, and check each scenario against known real behavior. Confidence: low. Sources: B50 p.101, 105-112, 119-120
- **Keep written scenarios and stories short and structured.** About one page in no more than four short paragraphs (who, goal and constraints, expectations, why this product), and user stories as role, goal and benefit. Confidence: low. Sources: B35 p.81-86; B25 p.40

## Workshop logistics

- **Run mapping sessions small and time-boxed.** About 5 people, 60 to 90 minutes for the exercise, and 4 to 5 meters of wall. Confidence: medium. Sources: B44 p.309; B96 p.75; B23 p.340
