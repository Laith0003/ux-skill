# microinteractions

- **Design each single-purpose moment as trigger, rules, feedback, loops and modes.** Treat setting an alarm, muting or logging in as its own unit and specify what starts it, what it is allowed to do, how it reports back, how it behaves over time and repeat use, and any mode it enters, so no part is left to default; at prototype level, name the trigger (click, hover, focus, scroll position), the action and the element that changes. Confidence: high. Sources: B14 p.33-38; B87 p.66-67, 78-80
- **Acknowledge every input.** Each click, tap and field blur needs a visible or audible response; people abandon an experience as readily when nothing happens as when they dislike what happens. Confidence: medium. Sources: B44 p.212; B79 p.161-163
- **Make every animated interaction interruptible.** A control must accept input mid-transition and reverse or retarget; ignoring clicks or disabling controls while something animates makes the interface feel broken. Confidence: medium. Sources: B42 p.68-73; B86 p.205
- **Set a small dead zone before a mouse press becomes a drag.** Treat movement within about 3 px of the press as a click; when one axis is preferred, commit to it early (about 4 px horizontally) and to the other only after a larger, cleaner move (about 8 px with little sideways drift), letting the first 5 to 10 px decide a constrained axis. Confidence: medium. Sources: B26 p.520, 522, 527
- **Show the outcome of a drag before the drop.** Highlight valid drop targets (scale them slightly on hover), drag an outline so the target stays visible, show an insertion marker, move neighbors aside to preview the new order and show a count when several items move. Confidence: medium. Sources: B26 p.515-520; B42 p.133-137
- **Auto-scroll near the edge only after a short dwell.** Start scrolling when the pointer sits within about 30 px of the edge for roughly half a second, and speed up as it nears the edge. Confidence: low. Sources: B26 p.518-519
- **Let the triggering button carry its own loading and success state.** When a save, submit or pay button morphs into a progress indicator and then a checkmark, feedback stays where the person is already looking. Confidence: low. Sources: B42 p.150-151
- **Bring key state onto the trigger itself.** Let the launching icon or button show progress, an unread count or what happens next, so people can decide without opening anything. Confidence: low. Sources: B14 p.46-47, 56
- **Make the trigger read as a trigger for this audience.** Use a control the target users already recognize as actionable; for unfamiliar audiences overemphasize it, even making a whole idle screen tappable. Confidence: low. Sources: B14 p.43-44
- **Animate a control in place when its function changes.** When a button switches role or a form branches, morph the change within the same space so people do not have to rescan. Confidence: low. Sources: B42 p.102-104
- **Confirm reordering with motion that makes room.** When an item is added, completed or reprioritized, move it to its new place while the others shift, so people need not search the list again. Confidence: low. Sources: B42 p.140-144
- **Use sound only to reveal state that cannot be seen.** Informative, natural sounds help, generic beeps annoy, and silence where a sound is expected is itself missing feedback. Confidence: low. Sources: B79 p.161-163
- **Keep haptic pulses short.** A press acknowledgment works at about 50 ms of vibration; longer buzzes read as alerts. Confidence: low. Sources: B15 p.115
- **Do not interrupt to say everything is fine.** A notification or callout that asks nothing of the user is a timing failure; alert only when action is needed. Confidence: low. Sources: B44 p.145
- **Allow at most one option that changes a microinteraction's rules.** More than one rule-changing setting turns a small moment into a feature; likewise cap visible suggestions at about three matches. Confidence: low. Sources: B14 p.89, 150
- **Decide dismissibility by removing the message and rereading the page.** If the page still makes sense without it, let it be dismissed, and store the dismissal so it never returns. Confidence: low. Sources: B07 p.61-62
- **Place a tip next to the control it explains and remove it on first touch.** One short tip per screen, cleared as soon as the person interacts. Confidence: low. Sources: B24 p.268-269
- **Snap slider values to round numbers.** Step to values people would choose themselves (100, not 98.2). Confidence: low. Sources: B07 p.136
- **Offer precision nudging for exact placement.** Arrow keys move a selection 1 px and a modifier moves 10 px; a fine mode can map about 10 px of pointer travel to 1 px of object travel. Confidence: low. Sources: B26 p.523-524
- **Delay tooltips enough that they do not flash on pass-through.** Show them only after the pointer rests on the control, rather than on every crossing. Confidence: low. Sources: B26 p.487
- **Add interactivity only when nothing simpler meets the goal.** Ask why before building an interactive element; if a static means achieves the same result, use it. Confidence: low. Sources: B71 p.146
