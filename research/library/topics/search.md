## Search and navigation together

- **Treat browsing as the main finding strategy and search as its partner, never its bandage.** On well-labeled sites search is used under 10 percent of the time and browsing starts about 9 in 10 tasks, yet both must exist on every page and work together; repair weak navigation before leaning on search, and budget time to tune any search engine rather than launching it with factory settings. Confidence: high. Sources: B93 p.234-235; B46 p.275-277, 281; B11 p.91
- **Link searching and browsing both ways.** Include relevant categories among the results, scope the box to the current section while someone browses, and keep scope choices to about 3 to 5 options on the results page rather than in the global box. Confidence: high. Sources: B93 p.285-286; B24 p.148; B77 p.82-83

## The search box

- **Offer one search box, in one fixed place, that searches everything.** Use a plain box with a button labeled Search or a magnifier, no clever labels or scope pickers in the global box, and at least about 20 characters wide since wider boxes invite the 2 to 3 word queries people now type; several boxes that each search different data confuse people. Confidence: high. Sources: B77 p.82-83; B93 p.274-279; B11 p.106-107; B46 p.281-282; B06 p.170-172; B04 p.54, 235; B58 p.98-99
- **Help people build the query instead of expecting a good one.** Provide autocomplete, recent and frequent searches and grouped suggestions; people who fear misspelling will otherwise scan long lists (over 90 percent of staff in one study did, some for over 4 minutes). Confidence: high. Sources: B26 p.574-578, 607-610; B24 p.144-146; B57 p.120-121; B11 p.106-107
- **Make suggestions instant or leave them out.** Suggestions that lag on slow networks or devices go unseen; display a progress cue when suggestions are slow, and use thumbnails only for lists up to about 10 items. Confidence: medium. Sources: B07 p.98-99, 236; B24 p.144-146

## Getting it right the first time

- **Design search to succeed on the first query.** First-query success sits near half, drops to about a third on the second try and near zero by the third, and over half of people quit after one failure; tolerate misspellings (about 1 in 12 terms), plurals and stems, and add spelling correction and did-you-mean. Confidence: high. Sources: B46 p.278-285; B93 p.254-255; B11 p.106-107
- **Mine the search log continuously.** Review top queries over about the last three months, zero-result terms, reformulations, click-through and time to first click; feed frequent terms into labels and missing content into the plan, and watch for repeated near-variant queries. Confidence: high. Sources: B11 p.70-71; B23 p.228; B35 p.317-318; B34 p.134-135
- **Route user vocabulary to your content with synonyms.** Misspellings, nicknames and product variants should return the same items (one retailer returned 2 results for a nickname versus 648 for the full name); synonym rings raised recall from about 20 to 80 percent at some cost to precision; show the preferred term so people learn it. Confidence: medium. Sources: B93 p.294-298; B11 p.85-87
- **Index destination content only.** Leave menus, listing pages, ads and boilerplate out of the index; in one example 5 of 6 results were navigation pages hiding the one useful page. Confidence: medium. Sources: B93 p.243, 246

## Results pages

- **Restate the query, scope, filters, sort and count above results.** Keep the query editable in the box so people refine without retyping, and show how many results remain so they know whether to narrow. Confidence: high. Sources: B93 p.260, 282-284; B23 p.490-491, 495; B46 p.278-285; B24 p.157; B20 p.740, 773
- **Rank by relevance and make the top results scannable.** About 93 percent of clicks land in the first 10 results and roughly a third on the first, many people choose within five seconds, so default to relevance, show titles and context rather than URLs or raw scores, bold the query terms in snippets, and group results by type. Confidence: high. Sources: B46 p.284; B06 p.74; B11 p.106-107; B93 p.258-259; B58 p.98-99; B23 p.499
- **Show 10 to 50 results per page, or progressive loading when people scan.** Fifty per page scanned fastest; offering View All or load-on-scroll suits scanning, while comparison and bookmarking need stable pages. Confidence: high. Sources: B46 p.284; B23 p.499; B24 p.157; B93 p.260
- **Write page titles that work as result titles.** Keep titles to about 60 characters with the first 40 naming the topic. Confidence: medium. Sources: B46 p.286, 618

## Zero results

- **Never let zero results be a dead end.** Echo the query to expose typos, then offer corrections, related searches, browse links or a site map and a way to reach a person; remove sort and filter controls that can only narrow an empty set. Confidence: high. Sources: B93 p.287-288; B04 p.38-40; B23 p.490, 495, 498; B35 p.317-318; B46 p.279
- **Design the no-results page first.** It is where the search conversation most often fails, so starting there surfaces the hard design and engineering questions early. Confidence: low. Sources: B04 p.33-34
- **Fall back to partial matches and show what was dropped.** Too many restrictive terms, second only to typos, is what usually empties a result set; run the query on a viable subset of words and visibly mark the omitted ones. Confidence: low. Sources: B04 p.40-44

## Filters and facets

- **Prevent zero-result filter states.** Show a count beside each option, update the remaining-match count live while filters change, disable options that would return nothing, put the most important filter first and always offer a clear-all exit. Confidence: high. Sources: B07 p.111, 247-248; B23 p.491, 495, 497; B24 p.173-175
- **Always show which filters and sort are active.** Display applied filters as removable tags or a summary bar, even when the filter panel is closed on small screens, and make AND versus OR behavior clear. Confidence: high. Sources: B07 p.105-106; B24 p.161-162, 168-176; B26 p.607-610; B93 p.282-284
- **Build facets that are objective, orthogonal and complete.** Use filters for clear traits such as price, size or type, not subjective ones; each item takes exactly one value per facet; name facets with telling examples rather than Category or Type; a horizontal filter bar holds about four filters before it needs another pattern. Confidence: medium. Sources: B11 p.87-88; B07 p.102-104, 107
- **Ask which meaning an ambiguous query has, then collapse to facets.** Show a prominent category chooser only when the system is truly unsure, keep it short (one store listed 14 categories, pushing results below the fold), and offer positive alternatives (other matching categories) rather than a negative escape link. Confidence: low. Sources: B04 p.203-212
- **Suggest refinements that keep the original keyword.** People trust related searches that extend their own words and distrust suggestions that push competing brands. Confidence: low. Sources: B04 p.201-203

## AI and context

- **Keep facets and a restated interpretation alongside language-model search results.** Say how the query was understood, summarize the result set and keep conventional facets (or extend them dynamically), since longer conversational queries are growing fast. Confidence: medium. Sources: B12 p.255; B11 p.107
- **Use location to sharpen results on mobile when it serves the task.** Location-aware results sharply raised engagement in one app. Confidence: low. Sources: B24 p.136
