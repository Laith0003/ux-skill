# motion

- **Never flash anything more than three times in any one-second window.** Flashing, blinking and tight high-contrast line or dot patterns can trigger seizures and migraines; WCAG 2.3.1 sets the three-per-second ceiling. Confidence: high. Sources: B91 p.240, 264; B18 p.183, 187, 259
- **Give a pause, stop or hide control to anything that moves on its own for more than 5 seconds.** Carousels, tickers, auto-updating regions and looping animation must be stoppable (WCAG 2.2.2); the better default is a carousel that does not auto-advance at all and opens on its first slide with clear controls. Confidence: high. Sources: B18 p.99, 183, 259; B91 p.240, 264
- **Do not autoplay audio or video.** Wait for the person to press play; sound that starts by itself and runs past 3 seconds needs pause, stop or its own volume control (WCAG 1.4.2), because it talks over screen readers, can cause anxiety and burns bandwidth. If autoplay is unavoidable, play once, muted, with captions. Confidence: high. Sources: B91 p.74, 245; B18 p.99, 185, 187, 259
- **Give every animation a stated job, or cut it.** Acceptable jobs are feedback, orientation, focusing attention, showing cause and effect, keeping context through a change, and demonstrating a gesture; motion added for delight alone, or because the tool makes it easy, distracts and costs build time. Confidence: high. Sources: B26 p.296-298; B14 p.117-119; B42 p.63, 179; B85 p.137-141; B74 p.151, 153; B78 p.59
- **Keep UI transitions between about 200 and 500 ms and always under one second.** Small state changes sit near 200 to 350 ms and long travel or bounces near 400 to 500 ms; below about 100 ms a change reads as a cut, and past about 1 s the result no longer feels tied to the action. Go to about 600 ms only when testing shows the motion needs it. Confidence: high. Sources: B42 p.73-76; B26 p.297; B86 p.205; B14 p.119
- **Make each animation as short as it can be while it still reads.** Start shorter than instinct suggests (halve the first guess, and consider halving again), stop trimming at the point the motion stops explaining anything, and allow bounce or elastic curves roughly 20 to 30 ms extra to resolve. Confidence: high. Sources: B14 p.119; B42 p.75-76; B86 p.205
- **Ease spatial motion instead of moving it linearly.** Curves that accelerate and decelerate read as physical weight, linear travel reads mechanical; elements entering from off-screen usually need only deceleration, and linear timing belongs to scroll-linked or progress motion. Confidence: high. Sources: B42 p.39-41, 54-55; B85 p.139; B87 p.84
- **Do not apply one house duration to every transition.** Scale duration with the distance and size of the change; a single value such as 300 ms used everywhere makes small changes sluggish and large ones abrupt. Confidence: medium. Sources: B42 p.73-74; B86 p.205
- **Keep motion faithful to the spatial model.** A panel that enters from one edge leaves by the same edge, and a selected element should grow or morph into its detail view with shared elements staying on screen, rather than one view fading out and an unrelated one fading in. Confidence: medium. Sources: B14 p.117-118; B42 p.98-102, 116-119
- **Use state-change animation to lower memory load.** Showing an element travel between states spares people from tracking the change themselves and teaches where things live, which matters most on small screens. Confidence: medium. Sources: B42 p.22-24; B26 p.296-298
- **Never let animation slow a frequent task.** A near one-second, multi-step reveal on main navigation is paid on every visit; keep showy entrances for rare content, keep build-in sequences well under 5 seconds, never block input during them, and end on the usable interface with no show-only elements left behind. Confidence: medium. Sources: B42 p.63-66, 167, 170-171; B26 p.297
- **Animate only the region that changed and merge rapid repeat inputs.** Start the response with little lag after the gesture and fold quick repeated inputs into one animation instead of queueing a chain that delays the result and can cause motion discomfort. Confidence: medium. Sources: B86 p.205; B42 p.68-73
- **Treat parallax and large moving fields as vestibular risks.** Heavy parallax and big surfaces moving across the view create a false sense of self-motion that can make people ill; keep them subtle and remove them under reduced-motion settings. Confidence: medium. Sources: B26 p.570; B36 p.272-273
- **Reserve looping or attention-grabbing motion for things that matter.** Movement captures attention involuntarily, so repeating motion should be kept for errors and alerts, not decoration or idle buttons. Confidence: low. Sources: B14 p.49
- **Stagger list items with small offsets rather than moving the group as one block.** A short delay between siblings produces a readable wave, signals that items are independent and spreads rendering work. Confidence: low. Sources: B42 p.45-46, 79
- **Apply the animation principles that carry weight in UI.** Most of the work comes from timing and spacing, anticipation, follow-through and overlap, arcs and secondary action: a small pull-back near the 5 percent keyframe reads as anticipation and a slight overshoot near 95 percent before settling reads as follow-through; any squash or stretch must preserve volume. Confidence: low. Sources: B42 p.44, 48, 53-54, 61
- **Decide what the motion says about the product.** People read weight, force and personality into how things move, so choose speed, overshoot and bounce on purpose to match the brand instead of accepting library defaults. Confidence: low. Sources: B42 p.25, 36
- **Bring a modal in from part of the way, not from fully off-screen.** Starting the entrance about halfway reads calmer than a full-height travel and finishes sooner. Confidence: low. Sources: B42 p.183
- **Plan and prototype motion early.** Mark where and how animation happens while still at low fidelity and test it early, since motion costs engineering time and is easy to bolt on badly at the end. Confidence: low. Sources: B85 p.137-141; B87 p.50-51
- **Drive animation from elapsed time, not frame count.** Computing each frame from the time delta keeps speed constant whatever the frame rate. Confidence: low. Sources: B15 p.91
- **Keep menu animations quick and give hover submenus generous room.** The opening should be noticeable but never disorienting, with large targets and readable text in the revealed menu. Confidence: low. Sources: B78 p.60-61
- **Provide a working path when transitions depend on script.** Animated page transitions need a fallback that still navigates when scripting fails or is off. Confidence: low. Sources: B78 p.62
- **In headsets, never move the camera without the user's own action.** Viewpoint motion the body does not feel is the main cause of simulator sickness: move at constant real-world speeds, avoid acceleration, prefer snap turns and teleports, and cut scene changes with a short fade to black while tracking stays live (full rules in xr-3d). Confidence: medium. Sources: B36 p.231, 250, 266, 269-273; B48 p.62, 148-152; B83 p.513-514, 550
