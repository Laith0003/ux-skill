## Cost and trust

- **Show the full cost, including delivery and price-changing options, before asking for personal or card details.** Surprise charges at the end, add-ons revealed only after personal information, and totals that do not add up drain trust; more than half of abandonments trace to forced registration or seeing the total too late. Confidence: high. Sources: B23 p.517, 525; B94 p.109; B38 p.56; B77 p.177-180; B08 p.263-264
- **Show shipping, returns, security and a contact route before card entry.** A visible phone number, return policy and help link are trust signals; hiding them spends the customer's goodwill. Confidence: high. Sources: B38 p.56; B23 p.474, 477, 518-519; B77 p.177-180
- **Surface incentives where the purchase decision is made.** An offer of free delivery that first appears at checkout never reaches people still weighing the purchase; in one test, offering it raised conversion from 3 to 5 percent. Confidence: medium. Sources: B44 p.37, 259-260
- **Offer the payment methods locals trust.** Where card ownership is low, offer payment on delivery, bank transfer and invoicing next to cards; content in the buyer's own language makes purchase several times more likely. Confidence: medium. Sources: B08 p.167-168, 350
- **Answer who you are and why to trust you on the first screen.** State the proposition quickly, support the top task (search, sign-in), and use concrete trust evidence such as reviews, heritage and contact details. Confidence: low. Sources: B23 p.474, 477
- **Format prices consistently and show what changed.** Use locale-correct separators and decimals, show unit price when quantity exceeds one, and show the original price struck through beside the sale price. Confidence: low. Sources: B62 p.132

## Checkout flow

- **Let people browse and buy without an account.** Ask for sign-in only when it becomes necessary (buy, save, share) and always offer guest checkout. Confidence: high. Sources: B24 p.81-82, 101, 379-381; B23 p.518-519, 570; B86 p.36-37; B02 p.232-233
- **Strip global navigation from checkout but keep a home link, progress, the cart summary and visible help.** Removing distractions keeps people on task while support stays one tap away. Confidence: medium. Sources: B23 p.518-519; B44 p.109, 169-170
- **Consolidate checkout and remove typing.** A single screen with expandable sections, address import, card scanning and one-tap purchase for returning customers cuts effort on mobile. Confidence: medium. Sources: B24 p.96-97, 102-104
- **Fix glaring basic-control problems first.** A confusing date picker in a booking flow blocked customers; fixing what a handful of interviews revealed repeatedly lifted revenue. Confidence: medium. Sources: B81 p.215; B94 p.100-101
- **Offer a detour, never a dead end, when an item is unavailable.** Show stock before Buy, hide or mark unavailable items, and suggest alternatives or later dates. Confidence: low. Sources: B44 p.183
- **Design the cart to hold one item or fifty.** Totals, edits and removal must work at both extremes. Confidence: low. Sources: B38 p.56

## Product presentation

- **Make listing thumbnails large enough to tell products apart, and show scale where size matters.** Tiny images lead to wrong clicks and back-and-forth pogo-sticking; a familiar object beside small goods enables size comparison. Confidence: medium. Sources: B04 p.104-111
- **Keep the add to cart action visible beside the key product details without scrolling.** Confidence: low. Sources: B38 p.54
- **Build the product template around the most complex product first, then check simpler ones.** Confidence: low. Sources: B23 p.508
- **Prototype faceted filters with a realistic catalog.** Facet interaction is deceptively complex and must be tested on representative data before build. Confidence: low. Sources: B74 p.31-32, 56
- **Keep vivid surrounding colors away from items whose color the buyer is judging.** Simultaneous contrast shifts the perceived hue of the product. Confidence: low. Sources: B70 p.28
- **Match type tone to price and product positioning.** Light, spacious type reads refined or healthy, heavy type reads cheap or indulgent. Confidence: low. Sources: B19 p.109, 111, 120
- **Show stock levels honestly.** A scarcity count signals demand only when it reflects real stock; a count that never moves reads as low demand or manipulation. Confidence: low. Sources: B44 p.220

## Measuring

- **Act on small conversion lifts at scale, and judge them with intervals, not single numbers.** A lift whose interval sits above zero is worth shipping for a high-volume store even if small. Confidence: medium. Sources: B94 p.32, 100-101; B58 p.496, 499
