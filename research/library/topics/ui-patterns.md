# ui-patterns

- **Follow established conventions unless the replacement is clearly better.** Standard placements (logo top left in left-to-right scripts, login top right, primary navigation across the top or left, the cart icon) spare people relearning; deviate only with a strong reason and a test. Confidence: high. Sources: B77 p.40-43; B27 p.130, 139, 141; B57 p.89-90; B78 p.17
- **Bind one operation to one kind of action everywhere.** The same control, gesture or button should do the same thing across the product and product line; arbitrary exceptions are hard to remember and cause errors. Confidence: high. Sources: B02 p.56; B83 p.168-169; B72 p.383; B78 p.17
- **Separate destructive commands from frequent harmless ones.** Never seat delete or erase-all beside routine actions, never give it a one-key shortcut or toolbar slot, and never style it identically to its neighbor. Confidence: high. Sources: B26 p.35, 213, 294-295, 414; B44 p.98-99; B54 p.52
- **Prefer recognition over recall, with a visible path to shortcuts.** Let people pick from lists rather than type from memory, and print the shortcut beside each menu item so novices can graduate; marking menus serve both groups on one path. Confidence: high. Sources: B20 p.735-736, 773-775; B83 p.174-175, 605; B40 p.30-31
- **Do not cap visible menus at seven items.** The seven plus or minus two limit concerns recall of transient items; visible menus are scanned by recognition, so short and long lists can both work if grouped. Confidence: high. Sources: B02 p.141-142; B11 p.100; B20 p.289, 732-733
- **Weigh number of choices against speed, and cut needless decisions.** More options lengthen decisions (Hick-Hyman law) and drain later choices; offer a curated starter set with a link to the full list and one clear primary action instead of many competing buttons. Confidence: high. Sources: B54 p.122-124; B44 p.73-74, 169-171; B26 p.227-228
- **Remember what people did and reuse it as the default.** Store last-used folders, sizes, values and choices; when a prediction is right most of the time (about 80 percent), act on it and rely on undo rather than asking every time. Confidence: high. Sources: B26 p.194-198, 224-228; B14 p.83-85
- **Do not start from a blank slate.** Begin from sensible defaults, recent templates, suggested options or fill-in templates and editable attribute chips, and let people refine instead of specifying everything. Confidence: high. Sources: B26 p.291-292, 418-419; B14 p.83-85; B54 p.137-140; B16 p.132-136
- **Ship defaults that work without setup.** Hide one-time and specialist settings, never require configuration before a mainstream user gets value, and remember that more options are not more control. Confidence: medium. Sources: B06 p.71, 74-75
- **Let the system absorb complexity.** Complexity is conserved (Tesler's law), so software should handle calculation, memory, pattern detection and search rather than the person. Confidence: medium. Sources: B14 p.86-87
- **Never collect or infer data that could embarrass or endanger the person.** Use context such as device, time and past behavior to prefill, within that limit. Confidence: low. Sources: B14 p.83-85
- **Keep automation from seizing control.** Silent rewrites of what people typed, unexpected renumbering or cursor jumps anger users; automate only where the need is obvious and the person is busy. Confidence: medium. Sources: B20 p.794, 805-808
- **Always offer an escape and a quick reset.** Every dialog needs Cancel; shared kiosks need an immediate route home and an inactivity timeout with a visible countdown that can be extended. Confidence: medium. Sources: B20 p.208-210, 795-796; B89 p.143
- **Never block entry with a modal that cannot be dismissed.** Make every pop-up closable, send focus into it, name it through aria-labelledby and aria-describedby, and when it closes, move focus back to the element that opened it. Confidence: high. Sources: B91 p.70-71, 264; B78 p.16; B86 p.159-160
- **Prefer inline alternatives to modals.** Use inline fields, disclosure, a separate page, tooltips or notifications for minor input; when a modal is justified give it one to three short verb exits plus a close control and return people where they were. Confidence: medium. Sources: B07 p.214-216; B86 p.159-160
- **Match notification placement to its scope.** Put messages about a task inline beside it, notices that affect the whole system in a persistent bar, and event-driven ones in an overlay. Confidence: medium. Sources: B07 p.63-64
- **Never leave a screen empty without a reason and a next step.** Distinguish still loading from nothing exists, explain empty lists and zero-result searches, and offer the action that fills them. Confidence: medium. Sources: B52 p.227-228; B58 p.329-332
- **End every workflow screen with an explicit next step.** Name the next action on the primary button for the current state, say plainly when nothing further is needed, and end a wizard where the person can actually finish. Confidence: medium. Sources: B58 p.329-332; B78 p.52-53
- **Remind people of the last step they are likely to forget.** Completion slips (taking the card, attaching the file, a confirm button below the fold) need a prompt or a layout that keeps the final step visible. Confidence: medium. Sources: B20 p.741-743; B81 p.224
- **Use forcing functions for critical steps, kept low-nuisance.** Interlocks enforce order, lock-ins stop leaving unsaved work (offering save in the prompt), lockouts block danger; annoying ones get disabled. Confidence: medium. Sources: B79 p.148-151
- **Remove a hidden prerequisite instead of explaining it.** Eliminating an unexpected required step fixes several problems at once and beats adding instructions. Confidence: medium. Sources: B89 p.147, 149-150
- **Do not impose order on checklists unless the task requires it.** Track skipped items and refuse completion until all are done rather than forcing a fixed sequence. Confidence: low. Sources: B79 p.194
- **Choose hub or wizard by task dependency and frequency.** Independent subtasks suit a hub (overview plus separate edit forms); ordered or rare tasks suit a wizard with Back, Next, save and exit, a step indicator and a summary page. Wizards of two steps are pointless and of fifteen tedious. Confidence: medium. Sources: B11 p.117-118; B58 p.319-326, 373, 606-619; B86 p.107; B14 p.80
- **Prefer state changes in one view over new screens.** Use progressive disclosure inside the view and reserve separate screens for rare, discrete steps. Confidence: medium. Sources: B14 p.80; B07 p.214-216
- **Use at most one mode, and make it obvious.** Modes breed errors; favor spring-loaded or single-use modes, and bring people back to exactly the state they left. Confidence: medium. Sources: B14 p.130-133
- **Never hide a high-priority action behind an invisible trigger.** Hidden gestures, right-click menus and hover reveals need a visible equivalent; secondary controls may appear on demand only if keyboard and touch reach them too. Confidence: high. Sources: B14 p.54-55, 93; B16 p.123; B52 p.142-143; B78 p.53-54
- **Make non-obvious gestures discoverable and forgiving.** Reveal them with animation, an icon or text, avoid shake, never collide with system edge swipes, and make accidental gestures undoable. Confidence: medium. Sources: B52 p.142-143; B48 p.79, 167-168
- **Do not give one element both single and double tap.** The wait for a possible second tap makes every single tap feel slow and invites accidents. Confidence: medium. Sources: B52 p.136
- **Use a select then activate model.** Firing an action the moment an option is chosen triggers by accident for keyboard users; let people choose, then confirm. Confidence: medium. Sources: B18 p.96
- **Insert disclosed content directly after its trigger.** Accordions and conditional fields put new content next in reading order and announce the change. Confidence: medium. Sources: B18 p.92, 197-198
- **Never use a drop-down to run commands.** Drop-downs choose values in forms; commands come from buttons, links, menus or action panels. Confidence: medium. Sources: B86 p.378
- **Make the active tab visibly come forward, and do not bury key content in tabs.** The selected tab contrasts and joins its panel; people often miss content hidden in other tabs, so keep critical content visible. Confidence: medium. Sources: B77 p.92; B23 p.466; B26 p.638-639
- **Never show selected state with color alone.** A toggle whose on state looks like hover or focus needs a second cue such as an underline, fill or icon change. Confidence: medium. Sources: B91 p.204
- **Pair icons with text, or use icon-only labels only for small learnable sets.** Most icons used to save space are misread and word labels are recalled better; unlabeled icons also cost scan time (about two thirds of a second per tooltip). Keep icon sets to about 8 to 12, 20 at most. Confidence: high. Sources: B93 p.174-175, 214; B28 p.274-276; B59 p.12; B35 p.243, 265; B46 p.687-697
- **Use one icon and label for one concept everywhere.** Do not mix hearts and stars for the same favorite action. Confidence: low. Sources: B07 p.118-119
- **Reduce icons to a few essential features.** Two or three graphical parts, drawn at standard sizes (16, 32, 48 px), read faster than detailed pictures. Confidence: medium. Sources: B46 p.687-697; B71 p.82
- **Test symbol and emoji meaning per platform and culture.** The same glyph renders and reads differently across systems and audiences. Confidence: low. Sources: B37 p.78
- **Label AI features by what they do, not with a sparkle icon.** Sparkles and purple gradients now signal an unfinished beta; name the outcome. Confidence: low. Sources: B16 p.123-124
- **Keep coding dimensions within perceptual limits.** Use at most about 3 to 5 sizes, 2 to 3 brightness levels, 2 to 3 line widths, 6 to 8 colors and 10 to 20 shapes when they encode meaning. Confidence: medium. Sources: B46 p.685-686
- **Place targets by Fitts's law.** Keep primary buttons near where attention and the pointer already are, make far targets larger, and avoid tiny targets, chorded keys and short time limits. Confidence: high. Sources: B57 p.93-94; B89 p.143; B69 p.173; B81 p.224
- **Use pagination when people need control and a sense of extent.** Infinite scroll suits endless browsing; pagination shows size, gives break points and marks the current page. Confidence: medium. Sources: B02 p.251, 531-533; B86 p.366
- **Build pagination consistently.** Summary on one side (results 11 to 20 of 55), controls on the other; keep first, previous, next and last visible but disabled at the ends, show the current page unlinked, and elide with ellipses beyond about 20 pages. Confidence: medium. Sources: B62 p.85, 291; B86 p.366
- **Give every filter an explicit Any or All reset.** Undoing filters one by one or through Back is slow and fights the mental model of all values. Confidence: medium. Sources: B04 p.147-150
- **Keep every facet reachable after a filter is applied.** Collapse or tuck options behind More, but never hide other filters. Confidence: medium. Sources: B04 p.151-152
- **Show four to seven values per facet with result counts.** Hide the rest under More, order by popularity for the query, show counts so people predict outcomes, and dim values that would return nothing. Confidence: medium. Sources: B04 p.149, 152-157, 165, 207-208; B86 p.89
- **Filter with links for single drill-down and with check boxes for multi-select.** Mixing both paradigms within one filter confuses people. Confidence: medium. Sources: B04 p.144-147
- **List discrete numeric attributes as rounded values, and use a single threshold for ratings.** Sizes or megapixels read as values, not ranges; ratings filters work as an and-up threshold rather than a dual slider. Confidence: low. Sources: B04 p.161-165
- **Treat sort as a primary finding tool labelled in goal language.** Few people pass page two or three, so sort acts as a filter; place it near filters and name options like cheapest first rather than relying on column headers. Confidence: medium. Sources: B04 p.185-194
- **Keep sponsored results integrated but identifiable.** Style ads to fit the page, yet separate paid from organic results by hue, position or rule. Confidence: low. Sources: B04 p.115-120
- **Make each feed item answer when, what and who.** Show time at useful precision (relative for live feeds), the event type and who did it, and cluster bursts of activity. Confidence: low. Sources: B07 p.113-114
- **Give every object three states: invitation, active, updated.** Each communicates what is happening, even when nothing is. Confidence: medium. Sources: B14 p.80-81; B78 p.52-53
- **Gray out options that cannot apply right now.** Disabled menu items reduce load and prevent wrong selections in feature-heavy tools. Confidence: medium. Sources: B57 p.88; B02 p.55
- **Only present actions the person can actually take.** State prerequisites, consequences and commitment around an action so nothing has to be guessed. Confidence: low. Sources: B33 p.65
- **Signal what an element does through its appearance.** Color, shape and size should hint at behavior so people do not have to test it. Confidence: low. Sources: B40 p.54
- **Keep text over images at a consistent contrast.** Use an overlay, lower image contrast, colorize the image or add a soft zero-offset text shadow. Confidence: medium. Sources: B47 p.202-206
- **Expose density and pace as user settings.** Culture, age and experience shift preferred density, speed, verbosity and media weight, so make them adjustable rather than hard-coded. Confidence: medium. Sources: B49 p.199-200; B21 p.281
- **Follow reading direction for sequence and menu orientation.** A left-to-right before and after reads backwards in right-to-left scripts; stack sequences vertically or mirror them, and orient menus to the script. Confidence: medium. Sources: B08 p.195-196; B49 p.144, 274
- **Express abstract units as tangible equivalents.** Turn milliliters into number of uses so people can judge quantity. Confidence: low. Sources: B25 p.64-65
- **Design templates with realistic content and character counts.** Real content types and lengths prevent overflow and odd gaps that lorem ipsum hides. Confidence: low. Sources: B39 p.24, 54
- **Fix structure when menu complaints accumulate.** Repeated reports about single misplaced items usually point to an incoherent overall organization. Confidence: low. Sources: B89 p.303, 312
- **Do not hide complexity by shrinking the screen or deepening menus.** Keep time-critical functions on dedicated controls and move only layered functions into menus. Confidence: low. Sources: B72 p.382-383
- **Surface AI features where attention already is.** Command palettes, actions on selection, empty-state suggestions and context-aware chips beat a separate tour; restate how a natural-language query was interpreted, let autocomplete insert without running, and flag unrecognized terms before submission. Confidence: medium. Sources: B82 p.59-61; B12 p.132, 135, 182-185, 202-203
- **Tune starter prompts to context and offer premade prompts for stressful tasks.** Suggestions should reflect the data source and recent history; conversational flows need follow-up suggestions, generative flows need one-click variation controls. Confidence: low. Sources: B12 p.147, 187-195, 200-201
- **In 3D and XR, anchor UI to the world, hand or body, never to screen edges.** Head-locked panels feel oppressive and can cause sickness, view edges lose resolution, and menus on a held surface give haptic support and fewer errors. Confidence: high. Sources: B36 p.200-203, 221-226, 275, 340-341; B83 p.580, 601, 608, 646-647
- **In 3D, point to select and grasp to manipulate, and cut degrees of freedom.** Ray-casting wins selection but fails on small or distant targets (use cone, bubble or progressive refinement); constrain movement to planes and axes with an option to switch constraints off. Confidence: medium. Sources: B83 p.412-423, 461-467, 632-633, 660; B48 p.169-170
- **Use platform gestures and buttons for reliability; gate speech input.** Gesture sets are invisible and fail some of the time, so keep them few and standard, prefer buttons for immediate actions, and require push-to-talk or a wake word. Confidence: medium. Sources: B83 p.349-350, 379, 586, 592-593; B48 p.79, 167-172
