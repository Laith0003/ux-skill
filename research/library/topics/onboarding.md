# onboarding
Input rows: 73. Output rows: 22.

Confidence note: mobile, AI and VR patterns are treated as technology-dependent. Rule 5 of sources.md lifts any lesson that three or more independent books support to high.

## First run

- **Deliver a first success before asking for anything.** Put no registration wall, long instructions, slow screens or ads ahead of the first win; let people look around freely and require an account only at the point of commitment (booking, saving), and greet newcomers with benefits and a clear next step rather than a sign-in box. Children abandoned games without a success in the first 30 seconds. Confidence: high. Sources: B86 p.33; B44 p.126; B33 p.55-57; B63 p.219-223; B08 p.288, 295-296; B78 p.12
- **Keep onboarding under a minute.** In one survey 72 percent of app users said finishing setup in under a minute mattered and many quit past two minutes; few people outside a lab will spend 5 to 15 minutes learning an app, and a worthwhile exchange should take less than sixty seconds. Confidence: high. Sources: B82 p.62; B52 p.99; B54 p.21
- **Never open on an empty screen.** Ship starter or sample content so the first run works (offline too), make seeded items easy to clear and exempt from plan limits, and treat the empty state as a first impression with one strong call to action and no filters that do nothing yet. Confidence: high. Sources: B52 p.278; B07 p.43; B47 p.234-236
- **Ask for permissions at the moment of need.** A location or notification prompt on first launch arrives before the user has a reason to agree and a denial cripples later features; never stack several dialogs at launch. Confidence: high. Sources: B44 p.36-37; B51 p.181-184; B24 p.366
- **Tell newcomers what happens next.** Sum up in a single sentence the product's identity, its value to the person, the reason to trust it and the first move; newcomers are often tripped up less by the task itself than by uncertainty about what each stage involves. Confidence: medium. Sources: B64 p.288-289; B33 p.50-51

## Teaching

- **Teach by doing at the moment of first use, not up front.** Cover only getting-started essentials, then offer brief in-place guidance the first time each feature is used; break hard tasks into small increments and unveil capabilities over time. Confidence: high. Sources: B24 p.254-257; B63 p.219-223; B51 p.181-184; B54 p.117-120; B48 p.161
- **Prefer one tip pointing at the first action over tours and overlays.** People dismiss welcome dialogs, walkthroughs, demo videos and see-through overlays alike; of five first-run concepts tested, only one hint that left the screen fully usable did well, and ignorable beacons that reveal one hint at a time let people learn at their own pace. Confidence: high. Sources: B24 p.245-246, 273-281; B42 p.132-133
- **If you use a tour, keep it to five to seven screens, skippable and re-openable.** Give back, next, dismiss and progress on each step, plus a replay option in settings; one ten-screen tour left no test participant able to finish the core task. Confidence: high. Sources: B26 p.415-417, 579-580; B07 p.32; B24 p.267; B77 p.168-169
- **Give hidden gestures a visible cue.** Tours do not save controls that lack signifiers; if users cannot rediscover a gesture on the next launch they leave, so add a hint or brief overlay that appears once or twice and then fades. Confidence: high. Sources: B77 p.168-170; B06 p.312; B04 p.311; B36 p.233, 245-246
- **Scale onboarding to how unfamiliar, how complex and how slow to pay off the product is.** A familiar, simple product needs almost none; start by streamlining the task or letting people practice on it, and reserve walkthroughs for counterintuitive concepts or doubting users. Confidence: medium. Sources: B33 p.77-78; B07 p.33-37
- **Name AI features plainly and introduce them when relevant.** A sparkle icon alone says nothing about what a feature does; introduce each capability at its moment of need and offer skippable re-onboarding after major changes. Confidence: medium. Sources: B82 p.57; B51 p.181-184
- **Let conversational users ask what the agent can do.** Do not front-load long capability speeches; answer what can I say with valid examples, offer a hideable tip line, and teach the navigation commands explicitly because users do not find them alone. Confidence: medium. Sources: B60 p.105, 181-185
- **Keep task help to about four steps per window.** Confidence: low. Sources: B46 p.646
- **Delay tutorial hint highlighting by 10 to 15 seconds.** Learners then think before clicking. Confidence: low. Sources: B58 p.198-199
- **Keep product demo animations to one task and about two seconds, with a text equivalent.** Confidence: low. Sources: B42 p.158-161, 173

## Fading help

- **Taper guidance as users become fluent, and bring it back after absence.** Optimize for perpetual intermediates: shorten prompts on second and third use based on recent regular use, offer got it or hide controls, record dismissals across devices, and restore help for features not used recently. Confidence: high. Sources: B26 p.267-269, 274-276; B63 p.74, 199; B41 p.70, 221-222; B14 p.136-139; B07 p.71-73, 79-80

## Momentum

- **Give people a head start and one next step.** Pre-filled progress nearly doubled completion in one study (34 versus 19 percent) and the pull rises near the end; a prompt naming a single next action beats a list of everything outstanding. Confidence: medium. Sources: B54 p.19, 116-120
- **Seed personalization with a few interest picks.** Asking new users for about five topics fills the first feed, and suggesting accounts by category rather than at random raised signup completion by 29 percent. Confidence: medium. Sources: B07 p.130; B54 p.139
- **Celebrate the end of a long first process and point to the next action.** Confidence: low. Sources: B25 p.79-80
- **Make the default path a small commitment.** When continuing costs one click and opting out costs two, people tend to proceed and honor the step they took; keep the default in the user's interest. Confidence: low. Sources: B54 p.114-115

## Immersive first run

- **Open immersive experiences with a calm acclimation scene.** Let users put on the headset, clear the safety notice, recenter and rehearse the real controls with a simple task before content starts. Confidence: low. Sources: B36 p.208-211; B48 p.142
- **Start content only when the user can see it, and keep recenter available.** Safety notices hold the view for up to 15 seconds on first run, so start timers after dismissal, recenter on entry, offer a fixed recenter key and pause when the headset comes off. Confidence: low. Sources: B36 p.88, 208-211, 229-230, 263
