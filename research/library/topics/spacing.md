# spacing

- **Put less space inside a group than between groups and at the edges.** Proximity is a stronger organizer than color or boxes on structured screens: label to field gaps are smaller than gaps between fields, heading space binds to its own content, and equal horizontal and vertical gaps imply false rows or columns. Confidence: high. Sources: B46 p.176, 183, 759; B26 p.444-445; B47 p.96-99; B52 p.208-210; B10 p.136-141
- **Build all spacing from one atomic unit and tokenize the scale.** Choose 4 or 8 px and make every gap a multiple of it (for example 4 between buttons, 8 between button groups), stored as tokens. Confidence: high. Sources: B26 p.444-445; B22 p.193; B47 p.71-73
- **Make the spacing scale non-linear.** Pack small values close and spread large ones, keeping adjacent steps at least about 25 percent apart, from a 16 px base; a linear scale makes small steps huge and large steps indistinguishable. Confidence: medium. Sources: B47 p.71-73; B22 p.193
- **Use white space to aid comprehension, not as leftover.** In a measured study, adding space between paragraphs and around the margins lifted comprehension by almost 20 percent; clutter weakens every element. Confidence: high. Sources: B46 p.187; B78 p.43-44; B71 p.66; B05 p.205-206
- **Form medium-sized groups.** A group of about six or seven lines and twelve to fourteen characters (five degrees of visual angle) is taken in at one fixation; many medium groups search faster than a few huge or many tiny ones, so break runs every five rows and never past seven. Confidence: medium. Sources: B46 p.182-183
- **Space paragraphs by about half a line, not a full blank line.** A full skipped line after every paragraph looks gappy; around half the leading keeps the column cohesive. Confidence: medium. Sources: B88 p.142; B10 p.175
- **Start leading at 120 percent in print and about 150 percent on screen.** Loosen it for long lines, large x-heights and long descenders, and tighten it for headlines. Confidence: high. Sources: B88 p.140; B92 p.60
- **Use whole-number leading that shares factors with the spacing system.** Auto leading produces figures like 10.8 or 21.6; choose values such as 8, 12, 16, 24 that let captions, body text and headings relate, and make module heights a multiple of the baseline so gutters stay even. Confidence: medium. Sources: B88 p.140, 143; B92 p.60
- **Start with too much space and remove it.** Adding room only until a layout no longer looks bad stops short; beginning with generous space and cutting it back lands nearer the mark, with dense data tools as the deliberate exception. Confidence: low. Sources: B47 p.66-69
- **Spend large white space only on what deserves it.** Isolation signals importance, so giving it to trivial content makes the piece feel unserious; use space around key elements to focus attention. Confidence: medium. Sources: B92 p.142; B71 p.66; B10 p.162
- **Use asymmetric space to move the eye, symmetric space for calm.** Equal margins and even spacing read static and stable; unequal margins and varied proximity create direction and tension, and near-touching elements read as tension while touching ones merge. Confidence: medium. Sources: B92 p.26, 77, 96; B10 p.50, 78, 83, 136-141
- **Make outer margins wider than gutters.** Margins larger than column gutters pull the content into one focused block. Confidence: low. Sources: B10 p.50, 89
- **Keep figure and ground unequal.** When positive and negative space carry equal weight, depth disappears and the layout goes flat. Confidence: low. Sources: B10 p.162
- **Pad tinted callouts by at least one em.** Text in a filled box needs about 1em of padding to read as contained, not cramped. Confidence: low. Sources: B59 p.9
- **Show fewer elements per view on small screens.** Reduce images and items per page on small formats so each gets room without zooming. Confidence: low. Sources: B05 p.205-206
