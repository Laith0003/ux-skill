# mobile

- **Make touch targets at least 44 by 44 pt on iOS and 48 by 48 dp on Android, about 7 to 10 mm.** Fingertips are 1 to 2 cm wide and small targets cause mis-taps; on the web WCAG 2.5.8 sets a 24 px floor (AA) and 2.5.5 asks for 44 px (AAA), so treat 44 px as the design target and 24 px as the hard minimum. If the visual must stay small, extend the hit area evenly around it. Older guidance of 30 px or 20 to 30 mm is superseded by the platform values. Confidence: high. Sources: B02 p.255-256; B86 p.297, 325-326; B52 p.132-133; B91 p.202; B80 p.32; B35 p.237, 242; B46 p.367-368, 457-458; B40 p.60
- **Leave inactive space between touch targets.** Gaps protect people with tremor, gloves, motion or poor light; where hit areas would overlap, split the difference, and make the whitespace around a control tappable for that control. Confidence: high. Sources: B91 p.202; B80 p.32; B18 p.98, 249; B52 p.132-133; B86 p.297, 325-326; B11 p.123
- **Never depend on hover on touch screens.** Touch has no hover, so tooltips, hover color changes and hover menus disappear; open tooltips on tap and make interactive elements look interactive at rest. Confidence: high. Sources: B77 p.163; B85 p.131; B23 p.568, 620; B35 p.242-243
- **Never disable pinch zoom.** People zoom even on responsive pages with large text; a viewport that blocks scaling removes their control (WCAG 1.4.4). Confidence: high. Sources: B91 p.197, 202; B80 p.26, 32
- **Stack mobile content in one column with the priority content first.** Start useful content within the top 100 px or so, avoid a layer cake of logo, ads, tabs and headers above it, avoid multi-column pages and wide tables, and put form labels above fields. Confidence: high. Sources: B86 p.299, 307; B35 p.238-240; B20 p.725; B06 p.300-301
- **Cut decoration on mobile, not capability.** Reduce marketing copy and secondary detail, but keep every task, the same content structure and a path to the full product; reprioritize instead of removing. Confidence: medium. Sources: B23 p.616-618; B38 p.77; B06 p.300-301
- **Put the most-used controls where the thumb rests.** On large phones the top of the screen is hard to reach one-handed; place frequent actions in the lower reachable zone and size distant targets larger, following Fitts's law. Confidence: medium. Sources: B52 p.119-120; B44 p.225; B02 p.637
- **Keep four or fewer touch controls across the screen.** Fat-finger errors rise past three to five per row, and fewer still fit when one control, such as a search field, is wide; tool and tab bars hold about four or five actions. Confidence: medium. Sources: B04 p.287-288, 294; B26 p.530, 533
- **Keep navigation shallow and labels short.** Few levels, the most-used functions first, two or three links at the top of a home screen, and menus under about ten items. Confidence: medium. Sources: B40 p.60; B86 p.316; B20 p.725
- **Minimize typing on mobile.** Prefer choices, autocomplete, sensible defaults such as current location, remembered values and one-tap clearing over free text. Confidence: medium. Sources: B11 p.123; B06 p.300-301
- **Follow each platform's conventions instead of porting one design.** Wrapping a website or copying another OS's patterns breaks expectations; keep the product's behavior and content consistent across web and apps while each surface uses its own native controls and navigation. Confidence: medium. Sources: B24 p.15, 360-361, 377-379; B11 p.123
- **Size kiosk targets for hurried, one-handed use.** Use at least 20 mm targets for close, unhurried users and larger at arm's length or under queue pressure, keep flows short, and align on-screen items with the physical parts they control. Confidence: medium. Sources: B26 p.563-564; B25 p.62-64; B46 p.367-368
- **Plan for the on-screen keyboard taking the viewport.** It can cover about half the screen, so keep the focused field and its submit action visible while typing. Confidence: low. Sources: B35 p.246; B23 p.624
- **Make tappable things look tappable and confirm the tap.** Links need a distinct look on small screens and a visible pressed state. Confidence: low. Sources: B40 p.60; B85 p.131
- **Keep critical status out from under the hand.** A holding thumb covers the lower corners, so put key status at the top or just above the touched item. Confidence: low. Sources: B52 p.135
- **Put frequent tablet controls near the upper side edges.** A two-handed grip reaches the upper sides easily and the bottom edge worst; thin bars at top or bottom tire the arm. Confidence: low. Sources: B04 p.325-332
- **Keep controls out of the scroll path.** Buttons in the middle of a scrolling region get hit by over-swipes; place triggers in corners or overlays. Confidence: low. Sources: B04 p.302-307
- **Use a hidden menu only when key actions stay exposed.** A hamburger hurts discoverability, so keep the likeliest actions and any update count visible outside it. Confidence: low. Sources: B25 p.89
- **Answer the top question on first open.** Show the one thing nearly every visitor wants, in large clear type, at launch. Confidence: low. Sources: B06 p.309
- **Respect standard gesture timings.** A tap registers when released within about 300 ms with little movement, a long press is held about 1 to 2 seconds, and outside games ask for no more than two or three simultaneous touches. Confidence: low. Sources: B52 p.140, 143, 147
- **Make phone numbers and addresses actionable.** Show a real tappable number, an address that opens maps and visible opening hours. Confidence: low. Sources: B35 p.236-237, 243-245
- **Do not force rotation or rely on pop-ups.** Require landscape only for video, keep pop-ups rare and always provide a working back control. Confidence: low. Sources: B35 p.242-243, 360
- **Keep the full query and active filters visible on mobile results.** A slim persistent bar with the whole query helps people resume after interruptions; refinements should not reset when keywords change. Confidence: low. Sources: B04 p.299-302
- **Design from the smallest screen up.** Group devices into a few size classes and define content and adaptation rules for each, covering widths from about 360 to 640 px on phones. Confidence: low. Sources: B40 p.59; B86 p.296
- **Load infinite-scroll increments in under about a second.** Otherwise use explicit paging. Confidence: low. Sources: B26 p.519
- **Hide on-canvas touch tools after a few idle seconds.** About 5 seconds without input is a reasonable point to fade them. Confidence: low. Sources: B86 p.313-314
- **Design ten-foot interfaces for a remote.** Large buttons, plain up-down and left-right navigation and no more than two columns. Confidence: low. Sources: B26 p.232, 565
- **Test with mobile screen readers.** Many screen reader users browse on phones with different gestures (single tap selects, double tap activates), so verify flows with them. Confidence: low. Sources: B80 p.39, 59, 68-71
