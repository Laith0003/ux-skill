# The book library

What 96 UX, design and research books teach, merged by topic and weighed, as input for ux-skill 5.0 (PLAN-5.0, Phases 3, 5, 6 and 7).

- `topics/`: 2041 lessons across 47 topics, merged from about 7,000 notes. Each lesson is rewritten in our own words, carries a confidence (high, medium, low) and the codes of the books that support it with pages.
- `conflicts.md`: where the books disagree, both sides, and the rule that settles it.
- `checks.md`: lessons that can become measured checks, with thresholds, method, fixture ideas and false-positive risk.
- `gaps.md`: what the books say against what the engine says today: agrees, partial, missing guide, or contradicts.
- `ACKNOWLEDGEMENTS.md`: the books, with thanks.

Nothing here quotes a book, and no lesson is credited to one person: every lesson is a blend of the sources that agree on it. Source codes B01 to B96 keep each lesson traceable without turning it into a citation of one author.

## How sources are weighed

### The rules, in order

1. **A standard beats every book on compliance.** WCAG, platform guidelines (Apple HIG, Material) and the HTML spec decide accessibility and platform questions. A book that disagrees with the current standard is out of date on that point.
2. **Evidence beats authority.** A lesson backed by a study, a measured test or real numbers outranks an opinion, whatever tier the opinion comes from.
3. **Authority beats count.** One tier 1 source outranks several tier 3 sources saying the opposite.
4. **Newer beats older on anything that depends on technology** (mobile, web platform, AI, VR hardware). On perception, cognition and color the old canon still holds.
5. **Agreement raises confidence.** Three or more independent books saying the same thing makes a lesson high confidence. One book alone is low confidence, unless it is a study.
6. **A newer edition replaces an older one.** An earlier edition defers to the later one on every point both cover.

### Lesson weight

| Factor | Points |
|---|---|
| Tier 1 source | 3 |
| Tier 2 source | 2 |
| Tier 3 source | 1 |
| Backed by a study or measured data | +2 |
| Each other independent book that agrees (max 3) | +1 |
| Technology-dependent and published 2018 or later | +1 |
| Technology-dependent and published before 2010 | -1 |

Confidence: high at 7 or more, medium at 4 to 6, low under 4.
