The Shade Divide: How Tree Canopy Shapes Summer Heat

By Priya Natarajan · June 14, 2026 · 9 min read

On a July afternoon, two streets a mile apart can feel like different climates. The difference, more often than not, is trees.

Researchers measuring street-level temperatures have found that blocks with roughly 30 percent canopy cover can be several degrees cooler in summer than nearby blocks with almost none. That gap matters most for the people who spend the most time outside: bus riders, delivery workers, children walking to school.

Where the shade goes

Canopy is rarely spread evenly. Older neighborhoods with mature street trees tend to keep their shade, while newer or poorer districts often have wide roads, parking lots and few planting strips. The result is a map of shade that follows the map of income closely.

"Shade is infrastructure. We plan for drainage and lighting; we should plan for shade the same way."

What cities can do

Planting is only the start. Young trees need water for their first three summers, soil volume to grow into, and protection from construction. Cities that track canopy block by block can target the streets that need it most rather than the ones that ask loudest.
